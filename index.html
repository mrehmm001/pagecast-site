It works out what the page is actually about.

Every block and image goes to the model with its position, its class names and where its links point. Nothing is removed by a keyword rule, so a figure in the sidebar survives and an ad inside the article does not.

Every removal keeps the model's own reason, listed beside you while you edit. Tick one back and the storyboard is rewritten to include it.

Nothing renders
until you've seen it.

The model drafts the slides and the narration. You get them as an editable storyboard, with a live preview of the finished frame beside every field.

Twelve layouts
Bullets, a pull quote, a big number, two columns, full-bleed photo. Change one and the preview redraws as you type.
The page's own pictures
Pick from everything the page had, at the largest size it offers. Nothing is generated, nothing is stock.
Narration you can rewrite
Each slide shows its word count and how long it takes to speak, so you can hit a length exactly.

It opens over
what you were reading.

No tab to hunt for, no download to find. The video plays on the page it came from, with subtitles, and saves to your Videos folder as an MP4.

Four pages, four videos, nothing touched up.

Each came straight out of the tool. They have sound.

Where the work
happens is yours.

Your Claude subscription
The extension talks to the Claude CLI already on your machine. No API key, no per-video charge from us.
Or nothing leaves the room
Point it at a local model through Ollama and use the on-device voice. Filtering, writing, narration and rendering all run offline.
Rendered on your own hardware
Your browser draws the slides, ffmpeg assembles the video. A one-minute film takes about three minutes.
Honest about the state of it
Today it is Windows-only and installs by script. That is the part we're fixing first, and what early access is for.
